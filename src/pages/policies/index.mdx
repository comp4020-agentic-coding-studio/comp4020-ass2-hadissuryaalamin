# Policies and support

The rules below apply across both assessments and every teaching week. An
assessment brief may link here rather than restate them.

## Late work

Written work submitted after its deadline loses 5 percentage points of the
available mark for each day, or part of a day, it is late. Work submitted more
than seven days after the deadline is not marked and receives zero, unless an
extension was granted in advance. The penalty is calculated on the mark the
work would otherwise have received.

## Extensions

An extension is a change to a deadline agreed before that deadline passes. To
request one, contact the teaching team in writing before the due date and
state the new date you are asking for; a request submitted after the deadline
is treated as a late-work case instead, not an extension. Extensions are
granted for circumstances outside your control — illness, bereavement, a
documented emergency — and are not granted for workload or time management
alone, in keeping with the course's own argument that time is a pressure to be
planned against, not an excuse. Where circumstances are ongoing rather than a
single event, ask for accommodation as early as possible rather than at the
deadline.

## Academic integrity

Both assessments ask for your own account of a dish you invented and your own
preparation of it. Submitting a recipe, a nutritional breakdown or a written
account substantially produced by someone else, by an AI tool, or copied from
a published source, and presenting it as your own reasoning, is treated as
fabrication. Group discussion of technique is expected — this course teaches
shared, checkable methods, not secret ones — but the written account and the
practical execution must be your own. Cite any source you draw a specific
figure or method from, the same way the teaching material cites its sources.

## Allergies and dietary requirements

Because the final assessment involves cooking and eating in front of others,
declare any food allergy, intolerance or dietary requirement to the teaching
team before week 6, when ingredient lists for the practical weeks are
confirmed. Where an allergen is present in a shared teaching kitchen,
equipment and preparation surfaces used for that ingredient are kept separate
from other stations for the remainder of the session, and the ingredient is
listed before tasting is offered to anyone. No student is required to taste or
handle an ingredient they have declared a reaction to; an alternative
ingredient or a written substitute is arranged instead. Declaring a
requirement does not affect marking — the assessment examines preparation
decisions, not which ingredients you were assigned.

## Kitchen safety

The teaching kitchen is a shared workspace and its rules apply to everyone in
the room regardless of experience:

- Closed-toe, closed-heel shoes and hair tied back at all times; no loose
  sleeves, scarves or hanging jewellery near a blade, a flame or a machine
  with moving parts.
- A knife is carried point-down, at your side, and announced ("knife") when
  passed or when moving behind someone. It is never left blade-up in a sink or
  under water where the next hand to find it can't see it.
- Raw poultry, raw red meat and produce eaten raw are kept on separate boards
  and never share a knife between uses without washing in between — the same
  cross-contamination rule week 10 covers as course content.
- Report a cut, a burn or a near-miss to the teaching team immediately,
  however minor; first aid supplies and a fire extinguisher are located at the
  kitchen entrance and their location is pointed out in week 1.
- Follow the teaching team's instruction to stop immediately if asked — this
  overrides any instruction elsewhere in the course material.

## Missing the week 12 live test

Week 12 is an assessed practical performed in front of an audience under time
constraint and cannot be re-created informally, so non-attendance is handled
as a deferral rather than a late submission. If you cannot attend for a
documented reason (illness, bereavement, a clash you could not have avoided),
contact the teaching team before the session where possible, or as soon as you
are able to afterward, and supporting documentation will be requested. A
deferred assessment is scheduled at the next practical session available and
examines the same thing the original does: whether your preparation decisions,
set out in your written account, held up once you cooked. Non-attendance
without contact or documentation is treated as a non-submission for that 60%
of the final assessment.
