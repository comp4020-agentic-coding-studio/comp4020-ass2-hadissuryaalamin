Which cut of poultry or fish goes into a dish is as much a nutritional decision as an anatomical one — this week's lecture explains why chicken breast, salmon, cod and duck behave differently at the knife; this exercise puts a number on what that anatomy means for composition.

## Exercise

Select each of this week's four items in turn — chicken breast, salmon, cod, duck — at the same quantity, and record the fat, protein and calorie figures for each.

<MacroCalculator data={week03} headingLevel="h3" />

Rank the four by fat content. The order should match the anatomy the lecture describes: a cold-water swimmer (salmon) stores fat through its flesh rather than under the skin the way a bird does, and a lean white fish that does little sustained swimming (cod) carries almost none. A ranking that surprises you is worth checking against the lecture's account of muscle use and fat distribution before assuming the figure is wrong.
