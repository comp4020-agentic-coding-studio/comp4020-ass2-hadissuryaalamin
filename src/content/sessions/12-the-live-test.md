Week 11 tested the course's thesis under controlled conditions, one variable
at a time, with no one watching but the group itself. This session runs the
same test once more, in front of an audience, on a clock, for the piece of
work assessed as the final: the public kitchen.

## What the session covers

This is not new material. It is a walkthrough of what the live assessment
examines and how a student should use the time before it: reviewing the
invent-a-recipe account written in week 6, checking that the ingredients
selected match what was predicted, and rehearsing the preparation sequence —
selection, storage handling, cuts, the order mise en place is assembled in —
that the examiner will be watching for before any heat is applied at all.

## Why the preparation is watched, not just the plate

A finished dish can look right and still have arrived there by an
unrepeatable accident, or look wrong and still have been prepared correctly,
with the difference explainable. The assessment's weighting reflects this:
execution under the time limit carries 20, since the clock is a condition
this practical adds rather than a discipline separate from preparation, and
an independent taster's sensory report of the finished dish, reconciled
against the week 6 prediction, carries a further 20; the preparation
decisions leading up to the dish and their correspondence with the written
account are the two heaviest components, at 30 each. This session's job is
to make sure a student walks in already able to narrate that sequence,
rather than discovering it under the clock for the first time.

## Rehearsal

Working from the invent-a-recipe brief, each student runs through their own
sequence of preparation steps aloud, naming the decision behind each one —
why this cut, why this storage choice, why this order — before touching an
ingredient. This is the same discipline week 8's timed exercise practised
against a guest framework and week 11 practised as a controlled comparison;
this session is the last rehearsal before both are done for a mark, live,
with an audience instead of a study group.

## Why there is no calculator this week

The live test carries no tutorial calculator: it is the rehearsal for an
assessed practical, not a new decision with a number attached, and the
course's exercises end at week 10 once every preparation decision they
cover has already been practised once.

## Afterwards

What leaves this session is not a grade — the grade belongs to the
assessment itself — but a checked plan: a sequence of preparation decisions
a student can state before they begin, so that whatever the live test finds
different is a result worth accounting for, rather than a surprise.
