Deciding how long a held item can safely wait before service is a preparation decision the guest lecture named but did not put a number on — this exercise supplies the number, using the same cold-chain rule that has applied to every perishable material this course has handled since week 3.

## Exercise

This week's lecture sorts preparation work into three categories: finished ahead of time, held in a stable state until called, and made to order. The middle category is the one with a real clock attached. Choose a held item below — a portioned or blanched vegetable, a stock, a sauce — enter the time it was brought to that state, and read off the time by which it must be served or refrigerated again.

<StorageCalculator data={week08Storage} />

Compare a held item kept at room temperature against the same item once refrigerated: the gap between a two-hour window and a four-day one is the entire reason "held" is a state a kitchen manages actively, rather than a place preparation work waits passively.
