This is the most detailed lecture in the course, and the only one where the
slide deck, not this page, is the primary artefact. What follows is a
summary of what the deck covers; the deck itself carries the labelled
detail — blade geometry, edge angles, hardness figures, the sharpening and
honing schedule, and the cuts — because a knife is demonstrated, not
described.

None of this week is cooking. A knife is used to prepare an ingredient for
storage, for a later step, or for the plate it will eventually reach —
never over heat. The distinction matters because a blunt or wrong knife is
where a preparation decision most visibly fails: an uneven cut cooks
unevenly weeks eleven and twelve later, which is the thesis this course
keeps returning to.

## Outline

- knife types, and what each is built to do
- blade anatomy: edge, bevel, spine, heel, tip, bolster, tang
- edge angle and steel hardness, and why the two trade off against each
  other
- typical blade lengths and weights, and what balance means in practice
- sharpening and honing as two distinct operations, with a stated
  maintenance schedule
- grip, and the standard cuts built from it

Open the deck for the detail. The week 7 session is where the cuts are
practised under supervision — the deck sets up what the session tests.
