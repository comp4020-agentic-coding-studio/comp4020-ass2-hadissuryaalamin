# Why prepare at all

SLOP1640 — week 1

Marisol Quaye

---

## The thesis

A meal is decided before it is cooked. Selection, storage, temperature,
cutting and sequence determine the outcome; the stove only reveals it.

Weeks 1–10 of this course are that decision, taken apart step by step.
Weeks 11–12 cook — and cook only to test whether the decision held.

# Time, measured

---

## Where the time actually goes

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics' American Time Use Survey, reported by
USDA's Economic Research Service, put average daily time on food
preparation, serving and cleanup at 37 minutes per person in 2014 — and
found that figure had risen over the preceding decade rather than fallen.

- Monsivais, Aggarwal & Drewnowski (2014) linked more time spent on home
  food preparation to indicators of healthier eating, not less
- more time in the kitchen did not track with less free time elsewhere in
  the survey data

The claim this course is built on is not "spend more time." It is: the
time that is spent decides the outcome long before heat is involved.

---

## Reconciling fast and good

Eating quickly and eating well are treated as opposites in most kitchens.
They are not opposites — they are only reconciled *before* the cooking
starts.

- a vegetable stored badly cannot be rescued by a fast pan
- a cut made unevenly cooks unevenly, however quickly it is cooked
- a decision postponed to the stove is a decision made under time
  pressure, which is where most of it goes wrong

Time is a constraint this course keeps returning to. It is not the
subject — the subject is what gets decided before the constraint bites.

# The shape of the semester

---

## Ten weeks of preparation, two of proof

- weeks 2–6, 9: selection and storage, by material — vegetables, animal
  protein, aromatics and oil, the tools that touch all of it, fruit
- week 7: the knife, the most demonstrative material in the course
- week 8: a guest account of preparation under professional time pressure
- week 10: food safety and the cold chain, the synthesis before cooking
  starts
- weeks 11–12: identical dishes from differently prepared material, then
  an assessed live test — the thesis, checked against the plate
