## 9. Defects found and left unfixed

**F1 — the week 5 attribution on the assessment page overstates what week 5
argues.** Not fixed. The Tester feedback paragraph says "the course's position,
argued in week 5, is that nothing about preparation depends on taste
perception". What week 5 actually asserts is narrower: its spec line is "you can
explain, *in terms of cells rather than taste*, why a crushed clove of garlic
smells stronger than a sliced one" — a requirement about the *register of
explanation*, not a claim that preparation is independent of taste perception.
Week 5's own subject is what aromatics contribute to flavour, and it cites work
on volatile composition and sensory profile, so "nothing about preparation
depends on taste perception" reads as contradicted by the lecture it cites. A
marker sampling week 5 and week 12 — the browsing pattern `CLAUDE.md` describes
— could land on both.

Left unfixed because the framing comes from the epic's decision 6, which is a
closed list this task is told not to reopen, and because the honest fixes both
sit outside this task's scope: narrow the sentence to what week 5 supports
(001's rationale), or strengthen week 5 (a lecture file). Recommend narrowing
the sentence to something like "the course does not mark a dish on whether it
was liked" and dropping the week 5 citation, or citing the week 5 spec line for
the narrower claim it actually makes.

**F2 — "the examiner" as a candidate tester makes the criterion partly
circular.** Not fixed. The paragraph offers "the examiner, or another attendee
at the same session" as the taster. If the examiner is the taster, the criterion
becomes "did the student obtain a report from the person marking them", which is
not really a student action the examiner can grade at arm's length. The
distinction from Correspondence is otherwise legible — the paragraph says
plainly that it is about a palate that is not the student's own, versus the
student's own comparison — but "narrower and more specific than correspondence"
mislabels the difference, which is the *source of the observation*, not its
narrowness. Left unfixed as 001's wording; a one-clause change ("another
attendee at the same session" alone) would resolve it.

**F3 — both assessment pages render two `<h2>How it is marked</h2>` headings.**
Not fixed, and pre-existing. `src/pages/assessments/[slug].astro` renders
`MarkingModel.astro` under its own "How it is marked" heading, after the body,
while both assessment bodies already have a `## How it is marked` section. So
the page reads: "How it is marked" (prose) → "The spec" → "How it is marked"
(the table). Confirmed on the untouched `invent-a-recipe` page too, so this
epic did not introduce it. Fixing it means either renaming the body sections
(content, both assessments) or changing the template — outside this epic. axe
does not flag it; it is an IA defect, not an accessibility one.

**Non-defect, recorded so it is not re-investigated:** screenshots taken with
Chrome's `--headless=new --window-size=390,N` show every page clipped at the
right edge, including the untouched homepage. That is an artifact of
`--window-size` not establishing a mobile layout viewport, not a layout bug.
Driving the same pages with `Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride` at 390×844
with `mobile: true` gives `scrollWidth == innerWidth == 390` on all five pages
— no horizontal overflow. Use device-metrics emulation, not `--window-size`,
for mobile checks in this repo.
