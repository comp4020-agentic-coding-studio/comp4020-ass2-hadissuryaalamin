## What the lanes must know

**1. `flex-grow: 1` on `.columns` does nothing. Do not rely on it.**
astromotion configures Reveal with `display: "grid"`
(`astromotion/pages/[...slug].astro:74`), so reveal.js stamps `display: grid`
inline on every `section`. A section is therefore a **grid** container with
`align-content: start` (deck.css:119), not a flex container, so `flex-grow`
never applies and rows are content-sized. `.columns` is as tall as its taller
cell and no taller. The epic's "it takes the slide's full remaining height" is
wrong; the two-column *width* is the entire benefit.

**2. A `.columns` cell is 544px, so the deck's 1.75rem body text overflows.**
This is the trap, and it hit on the first render. Wrapped text height scales
with roughly the *square* of font-size at fixed width — fewer characters per
line *and* full line-height on each extra line. Unchanged text that fits at
1120px wide does **not** fit at 544px. Measured on the prototype:

| Body step | Text cell height | Bottom | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.75rem (theme default) | 799.8px | 948.6 | **clipped, 229px off-canvas** |
| 1.35rem (the helper) | 497.9px | 646.7 | fits, 73px spare |

The first screenshot showed the third bullet sliced mid-line and the closing
paragraph entirely off-slide, while `pnpm check` stayed green — astromotion's
`deck-check.mjs` measures text `overflow` but the clipping is the section's
`overflow: hidden`, not an element-level overflow. **Screenshots were the only
thing that caught this.**

**3. Your vertical budget in a text cell is 571px.** The grid starts at y=148.8
under a standard `##`, and the canvas ends at 720. At 1.35rem/1.45 that is
about **13 wrapped lines** including inter-block margins. The prototype used 12
lines (3-line paragraph + 3 bullets wrapping to 6 lines + 3-line paragraph) =
497.9px. If a slide's text needs more than ~13 column-width lines, `.columns`
is the wrong tier for it — do not shrink the text further, and do not rewrite
the prose (epic DoD #11).

**4. Image height is set by the cell width, not by `max-height`.** With
`width: 100%` in a 544px cell, rendered height = `544 / aspect_ratio`, and
`max-height: 480px` only binds for images narrower than about 1.13:1:

| Source aspect | Rendered height | Clears DoD #2 (200px)? |
|---|---|---|
| 3:2 landscape (the common case) | 363px | yes |
| 4:3 | 408px | yes |
| 16:9 | 306px | yes |
| 2:1 | 272px | yes |
| 3:1 panorama | 181px | **no** |
| portrait / square | capped at 480px | yes |

So avoid panorama crops wider than about 2.7:1 in a `.columns` cell. Keep the
`max-height: 480px` anyway — it is what stops a portrait image running off the
canvas.

**5. `align-items: start` top-aligns the image with the first line of text.**
That read well on the prototype. No centring helper was needed, so none was
added.

**6. `.split-content` and `.image-credit` were not needed and are still
unused.** Nothing here required them.

## `theme.css` — exactly one class added

`src/decks/theme.css` now carries one new rule and nothing else. **No lane
edits this file** (epic parallelism rule 1); it is shared by all twelve decks
and task 001 owns it.

```css
.columns :is(p, li) {
  font-size: 1.35rem;
  line-height: 1.45;
}
```

Scoped to `.columns`, so every full-width slide in all twelve decks keeps the
1.75rem step the decks were written against. Specificity: the theme's default
is `:where(.reveal .slides) section :is(p, li)` — the `:where()` zeroes the
prefix, leaving (0,0,1) — so `.columns :is(p, li)` at (0,1,1) wins, and
`theme.css` imports `deck.css` at its top so source order agrees. Verified by
computed style, not by reasoning alone.

Lanes still size **images** with per-image inline styles, as the epic requires.
