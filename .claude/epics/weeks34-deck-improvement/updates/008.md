# Task 008 — week 4: choosing well, oxidation vs spoilage

## What was added

A new banner section, "# Choosing well", inserted between the existing
"## The danger zone, unchanged" slide and the "# Nutrition: ground beef and
ground lamb" banner (`src/decks/week-04.deck.mdx`, ~line 146). Two content
slides:

1. **"## Bright red, then brown: oxidation, not spoilage"** — explains
   oxymyoglobin (bright red, freshly bound oxygen) versus metmyoglobin
   (brown, further oxidised by air and store lighting) as two chemical
   states of the same pigment, explicitly stating this is "not different
   levels of safety". Paired with a photo of a fresh raw beef steak, bright
   cherry-red at the cut surface.
2. **"## What actually indicates spoilage"** — names the three real
   spoilage indicators FSIS actually publishes: slime/tackiness (a texture
   change), off odour, and the date on the label read against actual
   holding time. Closes with: "None of the three is colour. A brown cut
   with none of them is oxidised; a bright red cut with any one of them is
   not fresh, whatever its colour says."

The naive "bright red = good, brown = bad" framing is not shipped as a
safety claim anywhere in this section — colour is explicitly separated from
the spoilage checks throughout, including the closing sentence.

## Sourcing — fallback ladder

Rung 1 (matched fresh/declining real photograph pair of the same cut) and
rung 2 (a labelled diagram) were both searched for in good faith via the
Wikimedia Commons API before falling back:

- Multiple Commons searches for a genuine "fresh vs browned/aged beef"
  matched pair (same cut, comparably shot, both states) turned up nothing
  usable — Commons has isolated fresh-meat photos and isolated dry-aged/
  browned-meat photos, but no matched pair of the same subject shot the
  same way in both states, unlike week 2's onions.
- No suitable labelled diagram illustrating myoglobin oxidation (oxymyoglobin
  to metmyoglobin) turned up either — the search returned only unrelated
  anatomical/cuts diagrams.
- **Rung 3 used**: a single photograph, explicitly not presented as a
  comparison. No `.compare` layout, no before/after framing, no second
  image implying a paired contrast. The oxidation-vs-spoilage argument is
  carried entirely by the text (the two slides' body copy and bullets), not
  by the image — the image only illustrates what "bright cherry red" looks
  like, matching the "oxymyoglobin" bullet's own description.

Three single-photo candidates were downloaded and visually rejected before
the one used:
- an informal market-butchery scene — wrong register for the course's
  deadpan academic voice, could read as illustrating bad practice
- a cooked steak — wrong subject; every claim on this slide concerns raw
  meat
- a likely mislabelled/pork image whose EXIF `ImageDescription` field
  contained AI-caption-style boilerplate text — a softer AI-generation tell
  even though camera/timestamp fields looked plausible; rejected rather
  than risk it

Selected: File:Raw beef steak, 2011.jpg (Wikimedia Commons), photographer
Jellaluna, CC BY 2.0. Full EXIF confirmed via Commons `imageinfo` API:
Make=PENTAX, Model="PENTAX K-x", DateTimeOriginal=2011:05:06 21:20:24,
ExposureTime=1/13, FNumber=4.0, ISO=800, FocalLength≈24mm — realistic
amateur-DSLR capture data, no AI-generation tell. Original 3936×2624,
resized locally (Python PIL, LANCZOS) to 960×640, saved as
`src/decks/assets/week-04/beef-steak-fresh.jpg`. Credit line appended to
`src/decks/assets/week-04/SOURCES.md` after the existing 7 entries, in the
established format.

Real alt text: "A raw beef steak on a cutting board, its cut surface a
bright cherry-red where it has been freshly exposed to air."
